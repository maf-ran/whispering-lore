Šajkača

garment · Serbia · Slavic

The šajkača is the traditional Serbian cap, a rounded woollen cap with a raised crown whose four points fold into a characteristic V shape, made from a single piece of cloth and worn without a visor. Its name honours the šajkaši, the eighteenth-century Serbian river troops who crewed the šajka boats of the Danube and Sava under Habsburg command and who first wore the shape. From the nineteenth century the šajkača spread beyond the river garrisons to Serbian soldiers, teachers, priests and peasants, becoming a badge of Serbian national identity, worn in the Balkan Wars and the First World War a
