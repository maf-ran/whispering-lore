Chac Mool

religious object · Mexico · Mesoamerica

A chac mool is a reclining stone figure with its head turned to one side, knees drawn up and a flat basin held on its stomach, set at the entrances of temples across Mesoamerica from Toltec Tula to Aztec Tenochtitlan and Maya Chichen Itza. The basin served as a receptacle for offerings, and in Aztec contexts the figure is especially linked to the rain god Tlaloc and to the hearts and blood of sacrifice.
