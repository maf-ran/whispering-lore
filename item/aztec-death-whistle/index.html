Aztec Death Whistle

musical instrument · Mexico · Mesoamerica

The Aztec death whistle, sometimes called ehecachichtli or skull whistle, is a small clay whistle about three to five centimetres long, modelled as a human skull, whose internal chambers produce a shrill, wind-like and unsettling tone. The most famous examples were excavated in the late 1990s at Tlatelolco, clasped in the hands of a sacrificed man buried before a temple of the wind god Ehecatl, and they have been linked to both Ehecatl and the death god Mictlantecuhtli.
