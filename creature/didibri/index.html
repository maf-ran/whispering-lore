Didibri

dragon · Suriname · South America

Didibri are dragon-like creatures from Surinamese folklore, derived from the Sranan Tongo word for devil or dragon. These fearsome beings appear in the oral traditions of Suriname's diverse population, including Creole, Maroon, and Indigenous communities. The most famous tale, documented by folklorists Melville and Frances Herskovits in the early 20th century, tells of a twelve-tailed dragon named Didibri who transforms himself into a handsome human — calling himself Je Man — to woo a human woman.
