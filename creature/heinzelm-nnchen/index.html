Heinzelmännchen

household-spirit · Germany · Germanic

The Heinzelmännchen are legendary household spirits from Cologne, Germany, akin to brownies or hobgoblins in other European traditions. These diminutive helpers were said to perform all the household and workshop tasks of Cologne's citizens during the night, allowing the lazy inhabitants of the city to enjoy their days in complete leisure. The legend was first recorded in writing by Cologne teacher Ernst Weyden in 1826 and later popularized by poet August Kopisch in 1836, whose ballad about the Heinzelmännchen became immensely popular and cemented these creatures in German folklore.
