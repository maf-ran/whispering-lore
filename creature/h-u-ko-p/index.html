Hóu-koô-pô

animal-spirit · Taiwan · East Asia and Pacific

Hóu-koô-pô (also known as Hóo-koo-pô, 'Tiger Aunt' or 'Tiger Grandma') is a terrifying figure from Taiwanese folklore — a tiger spirit from the mountains that transforms into an old woman to prey on children. The story is a classic cautionary tale used by Taiwanese parents to teach children about the dangers of trusting strangers and the importance of obedience. With over thirty-two recorded variants across Taiwan, the tale is classified by folklorists as AT type 333C (The Tiger Grandma), related to but distinct from the European Little Red Riding Hood.
