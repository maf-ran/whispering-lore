Futakuchi-Onna Variant

monster · Cameroon · Central Africa

This Cameroonian variant of the two-mouthed woman draws from Central African witchcraft traditions, where the concept of hidden bodily anomalies as markers of supernatural power is common. Unlike the Japanese Futakuchi-onna, which typically results from a miserly husband starving his wife, the Cameroonian variant is associated with the deliberate acquisition of occult power through black magic. In Cameroon's diverse spiritual traditions, witches are believed to gain extraordinary abilities through pacts with malevolent spirits, often marked by physical transformations.
